# Gaisberg: art brief

Salzburg's home mountain. The route climbs 9.8 km from Koppl Professorfeld (572 m) via Guggenthal and the
Gaisberg Höhenstraße (L108) to the summit loop at about 1275 m. The rider goes round the summit roundabout (202 m)
and back down, endlessly. Facts and sources: `research/gaisberg/visual-reference.md` and `research/gaisberg/geodata.md`.

**Lead-in** (on by default, `scripts/bake-leadin.py`): 2.9 km of easy riding from Gnigl in Salzburg up the B158
through the two Obergnigl switchbacks to Guggenthal, where it joins the route on the B158 about 350 m after the
route's own start (which sits on a side street). The trainer stays easy (a third of the gradient, at most 2 %);
the climb, its timer and its record start at the join. With the lead-in on, the endless turnaround at the bottom
is a 676 m block in Gnigl (Linzer Bundesstraße, Franz-Hattinger-Straße, Versorgungshausstraße). Km below are
route km without the lead-in (the `?ride=` test URLs use them); lead-in km are marked L.

**Mood:** a clear October day on the city's own mountain. Valley farmland and villages, then mixed forest, alm
meadows with cows, and an open summit plateau looking over the Salzburg basin and the Alps. The signature is the
**Nebelmeer** (`time=inversion`): fog lies over the basin while the summit is in sun.

**Hero categories** (scorecard, need 4+): vegetation, road and roadside, sense of place.

## Look targets

**Road.** Mid-grey asphalt with a fine grain and few cracks. Dashed white centre line and solid white edge line.
Grass meets the asphalt directly, with at most a narrow gravel seam. Galvanised W-beam guardrail on the valley side
of bends and drop-offs. White Leitpflöcke with a black band: red reflector on the right, white on the left (StVO
§57). Red-white chevron boards on hairpins. A convex mirror at the Gersbergalm junction.

**Meadows.** Bright yellow-green, mown or grazed, low and even. Darker green under trees, dry straw patches and
worn tracks on the summit. Slight undulation. Never tall spiky blades.

**Forest.** Beech and mixed broadleaf on the lower slopes: yellow, orange and brown in October, not uniform lime.
Spruce higher up: narrow, dark, branches drooping in curtains. An open understorey with light between the trunks,
brown leaf litter, pale grey limestone slabs and outcrops with roots over them. The forest edge is irregular, with
gaps.

**Rock.** Cool pale-grey limestone, in outcrops and bands, never evenly scattered dots. From below, the summit dome
shows pale limestone bands on its upper face (Kapaunwände).

**Buildings.** Salzburg Flachgau style. Alm inns and hotels have white render, timber upper floors and balconies,
pitched roofs with wide eaves, and flower boxes. Farmhouses are long with a pitched roof. No apartment blocks, no
towers, nothing on the road shoulder.

**Summit.** An open grass plateau with gravel paths and a gravel apron, groups of tall spruces, the transmitter
compound (pale modern building and fence) and the 100 m red-white lattice mast. The asphalt parking lot is separate
from the loop: cars and the Albus bus park in the lot, never on the loop. Benches, panorama boards and the summit
cross.

**Life.** Brown-and-white cows on the pastures (the board photo shows Fleckvieh). Hikers on the trails,
paragliders launching and circling over the summit, light car traffic, and the Albus 151 bus. Jackdaws at the top.

**Light.** Default noon: a high sun, a saturated blue sky with cirrus, and short shadows. Strong aerial
perspective over the basin, with Untersberg and Hoher Göll as blue silhouettes. Nebelmeer: a soft, bright fog top
with some shading, and trees near the road staying crisp.

## Open issues

From `research/gaisberg/qa-sweep.md` (2026-10-11). Each needs re-checking in the next review.

- Grass built from spiky cards with hard edges (verges at 0.3, 1.3, 4.3 and 9.3 km).
- Tree crowns read as cut-out cards; some clones.
- Speckle noise on verges.
- Summit meadow too airbrushed.
- Parked cars and the bus straddling the loop edge.
- Rocks evenly scattered.
- Placeholder buildings: a box at 1.3 km, a tower-like building at 7.3 km.
- Inversion fog flat on the chase view.
- Lead-in (Gnigl to Guggenthal) has no reference photos on the board yet and was built from OSM and the canopy
  model only. First frames (2026-10-11): suburban Gnigl buildings are the generic Alpine set; a rectangular pond near
  the switchbacks reads as bright as a pool.
- Fixed since: the grey shoulder smear (road shoulder alpha), floating trunks (placed on `groundHeight`). The
  square summit shadow is the real transmitter platform, with shadow edges now faded.
